So where would your own 20 years fall? Here is a line to measure them against. Averaged over every month since 1934, the 3-month bill paid 3.42 percent. For a roll to end above double, the bill rate has to average about 3.47 percent a year across all 20 years, which is the bond's doubling measured the way bill rates are quoted, so the long-run average sat just under the line. The middle roll of all 873 ended at 2.097 times the money, barely over the line. For money like Dana's, the question isn't the rate on the day it starts; it's whether the bill rate averages above that line over all 80 bills, a rule that matched the result in every one of the 873 starts. Nobody can say where that average will land, and today's rate can't either. The line doesn't say which to pick; it says what the roll has to do to beat the promise.
