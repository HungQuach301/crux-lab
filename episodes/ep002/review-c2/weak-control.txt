Or has it? Here is what that division leaves out. Maya's old loan already has 35 payments behind it. A refinance starts a fresh 30 years, so the new loan pays the balance down more slowly than the old one would have. Early payments are mostly interest. Maya's old loan was just starting to pay down real principal, and the new one starts that schedule over. So count what she still owes on each loan, month by month. After 24 months, the new loan leaves her owing $1,133 more than the old one would have. So, counting what she still owes, break-even is not 24 months. It is 30. Most calculators stop at the monthly saving. The balance keeps the score for longer. The smaller the cut, the bigger that gap. At a cut of 0.25 points, the division would promise 38 months.
