30 year mortgage paid in 15 years, or a real 15-year mortgage? At the survey rates of the week of October 8, 2026, the 30-year's lower-payment fallback costs $120 a month.

Tessa (ILLUSTRATIVE) borrows $320,000 in the week of October 8, 2026: a 15-year mortgage at 6.73%, or a 30-year at 7.40% paid off in 15 years anyway, keeping the 30-year's lower required payment, about $613 a month lower, as a fallback for a bad month. Paying the 30-year in 15 years takes $2,948 a month against the 15-year's $2,828: $120 a month, $21,622 over 15 years. That is the stack, the price of the fallback.

We replayed 1,833 weeks of the Freddie Mac weekly mortgage survey, from August 1991 to the week of October 8, 2026, and priced the same plan in every week. The stack ranged from $6,097 to $29,502, with a typical week at $16,631; fewer than 1 in 5 weeks cost as much as the week of October 8, 2026. At Tessa's rates of the week of October 8, 2026, paying the 30-year exactly what the 15-year costs ends about 15 months late; finishing in 180 months takes $733 a month on top of the required payment. The same extra sent as $8,792 once a year ends in 184 months and charges about $8,200 more in interest than paying it monthly.

Then the bar: each extra dollar of principal on Tessa's 30-year saves interest at exactly the loan rate, 7.40% a year, before taxes. That is interest no longer charged, not an investment return. How we know this (method and sources)
- Data: Freddie Mac Primary Mortgage Market Survey, weekly US averages of the 30-year and 15-year fixed rates, 1,833 paired weeks from August 1991 to the week of October 8, 2026. The survey describes borrowers with strong credit and a large down payment; real offers differ in points, fees and terms.
- Sources: MORTGAGE30US and MORTGAGE15US, retrieved from FRED, Federal Reserve Bank of St. Louis; https://fred.stlouisfed.org/series/MORTGAGE30US and https://fred.stlouisfed.org/series/MORTGAGE15US (cross-check: OBMMIC30YF and OBMMIC15YF, retrieved from FRED, Federal Reserve Bank of St. Louis; https://fred.stlouisfed.org/series/OBMMIC30YF and https://fred.stlouisfed.org/series/OBMMIC15YF).
- Method: in every week, the same $320,000 priced as a 15-year loan and as a 30-year loan paid off in 15 years, at that week's own rates; the stack is the difference between the two interest totals.
- Payments are principal and interest only: no property taxes, homeowners insurance, private mortgage insurance (PMI) or tax deductions.
- The bar is the loan's own rate: interest no longer charged on each extra dollar of principal, before taxes. It is not an investment return, and this video does not test how any investment compares, once risk and taxes count.
- What a lower required payment is worth in a job loss or an emergency is not measured.
- Tessa and Leo are ILLUSTRATIVE. Tessa's numbers hold only at the survey rates of the week of October 8, 2026 on $320,000; another offer gives another stack.

US only · history, not a forecast. Not advice on which loan to take, or on whether to invest.
