# C1 — logline (máy; đề tài khuyến nghị #17 `topics-r2/machine/debt-2/`, format `101`)

Lý do khuyến nghị: D-007 (thí điểm 101, trụ vay/mortgage trước); một khái niệm (PMI + mốc 80 %) + một thí nghiệm nhỏ trên dữ liệu thật; khác Tập 1 (tái cấp vốn) và Tập 4 (thuế bán nhà); dữ liệu FRED công khai, SHA khớp hồ sơ, 17/17 câu đúng. Ứng viên khác cho G1: #13 debt-1 (15 năm hay 30 năm trả trong 15), #9 debt-4 (trả 1 điểm để có 6.75%; gần Tập 1).

**L1 (khái niệm trước):** Put 10% down on a home and your lender adds private mortgage insurance until you owe less than 80% of the home's value. On the payment schedule alone, a loan at September 2026's average rate of 6.86% takes about eight years to get there. We replay 25 years of US home prices and mortgage rates, one purchase month at a time, to measure how long 10%-down buyers actually took to reach 80% on paper, and why three buyers who started in different years got very different answers. It won't tell you whether to buy or wait: reaching 80% on paper is not the same as getting the insurance removed, and lenders set their own rules.

**L2 (câu hỏi người xem trước):** You've saved 10% for a first home. Buy now and pay mortgage insurance, or keep renting until you have 20%? Most buyers expect close to a decade of insurance, because that's how long the payment schedule takes to reach 80% of the price. We replay every purchase month from 1991 to 2016 of US home prices and mortgage rates to see how long 10%-down buyers really took to reach 80% of their home's value on paper: the usual case, and the bad ones. It won't tell you whether to buy or wait: equity on paper isn't the same as getting the insurance removed, and no home tracks the national average exactly.
