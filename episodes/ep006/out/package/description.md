Does an annuity check that rises 2% a year keep up with US consumer prices? We replayed every 20-year stretch of retirement starting from January 1947 to August 2006, 715 stretches, and asked whether a check rising 2% a year still bought at least what its own first check bought. It kept up in 17 of the 715, all starting between September 1947 and January 1949. In the typical stretch, the rising check ended at 80.7% of its first check's buying power. US only · history, not a forecast. This video does not say which check, raise, product or company to choose, and it does not compare the dollars the checks pay out over a lifetime.

Method and sources
- Prices: CPI-U, all items, U.S. city average, all urban consumers, not seasonally adjusted (BLS via FRED CPIAUCNS, https://fred.stlouisfed.org/series/CPIAUCNS), monthly, through August 2026. Cross-check of the 12-month change: CPI-U seasonally adjusted (FRED CPIAUCSL, https://fred.stlouisfed.org/series/CPIAUCSL).
- The check rises 2% once a year, at each anniversary. Buying power = the check divided by the price index, compared with the first check. A stretch kept up when, after 20 raises, the check still bought at least what its first check bought. Starting months January 1947 to August 2006: 715 stretches of 20 years (and 655 of 25 years, none of which kept up). The stretches overlap, so they are not 715 separate tests.
- Other price measures: CPI-W, the index behind Social Security's yearly raises (FRED CWUR0000SA0, https://fred.stlouisfed.org/series/CWUR0000SA0): 2.9% of stretches kept up. PCE price index (BEA via FRED PCEPI, https://fred.stlouisfed.org/series/PCEPI, from 1959): 19.2%.
- The level check is measured against its own first check, not in dollars. How much smaller a rising check starts depends on the insurer and is not modeled.
- Data: FRED, Federal Reserve Bank of St. Louis (public domain, citation requested).

Not modeled: annuity pricing and the size of either check, taxes and fees, the insurer, a retiree's own basket (health care and housing can weigh differently for older households), and the dollars paid out over a lifetime.

US only · history, not a forecast. Not advice.
