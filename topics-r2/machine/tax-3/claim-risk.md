# tax-3 claim risk: rental losses on $130,000 of wages

**Phrasings that would overstate the claim**
- "You can't deduct rental losses." At $130,000 a couple can deduct up to $10,000 a year. Losses above that are suspended and carried forward, not lost. They are released when the property is sold or against future passive income.
- "The median family is now phased out." The median household income ($87,460 in 2025) is still below the $100,000 start. Say "the line now sits just above the typical household".
- "Rentals are a bad investment." The episode measures one tax rule, not rental returns.

**What the episode must not say**
- No advice. Do not say "don't buy a rental" or "become a real estate professional".
- No forecast of incomes, prices or law changes. Do not predict that Congress will index the lines.

**Data and rule limits**
- The income measure for the allowance is modified AGI, not wages. Pre-tax 401(k) deferrals lower it, and some items are added back.
- CPI-U 2025 average uses 11 months (October 2025 was not published). Median household income is a survey estimate with methodology breaks, and it covers all households, not just married couples.
- Real estate professionals, short-term rentals and material participation follow different rules, which the episode does not cover.
- The rule is quoted from 26 U.S.C. 469(i)(2) and (i)(3)(A).
