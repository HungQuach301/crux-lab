# tax-2 claim risk: the $1,000 emergency withdrawal vs a credit card

**Phrasings that would overstate the claim**
- "The 401(k) withdrawal is cheaper." That holds only if the card balance would take longer than the crossover (12 months at the May 2026 average rate, 12% bracket). A card cleared in a few months costs less than the tax.
- "It's tax-free." The withdrawal avoids the 10% additional tax but is still taxed as income, unless it is repaid within three years.
- "Your card costs 22.15%." That figure is a national average on accounts charged interest. Individual APRs vary widely.

**What the episode must not say**
- No advice. Do not say "use your 401(k)" or "never touch your 401(k)". Show the month count and the assumptions.
- No forecast of card rates.
- Do not imply every plan offers this withdrawal. Plans may choose not to.

**Data and rule limits**
- TERMCBCCINTNS is a quarterly average for commercial-bank card accounts assessed interest. It excludes fees and promo rates.
- The comparison excludes the investment growth the $1,000 would have earned inside the 401(k) (that raises the withdrawal's cost) and state income tax.
- Rules are quoted from 26 U.S.C. 72(t): no 10% additional tax, one withdrawal per calendar year, $1,000 cap, repayment within three years. A repeat withdrawal within three years requires repayment or equal new contributions.
