# retire: discarded candidates (topics-r2, machine side)

Candidates considered: 16. Kept: 4 (retire-1 prepay a funeral vs save the money, retire-2 give a down payment now vs in 10 years, retire-3 callable 5-year CD at a premium vs a plain CD, retire-4 travel early in retirement vs spread over 10 years). Dropped: 12.

1. Withdraw 4% a year from a portfolio of only T-bills and CDs: answered-with-data (all-bond and all-cash failure rates of the 4% rule, e.g. Of Dollars and Data "What is the Safe Withdrawal Rate", Best Interest "The 4 Percent Rule").
2. Keep a fixed $1 million retirement target or raise it with prices: answered-with-data (InvestmentNews "$1M is not what it used to be", inflation-calculator pieces with historical figures).
3. Keep near-retirement safe money in an intermediate bond fund or T-bills: answered-with-data (Liberty Street Economics "The Bond Market Selloff in Historical Perspective", Bogleheads "Rolling ladders versus bond funds").
4. Budget retirement spending at today's level or plan for it to fall with age: answered-with-data (BLS "Spending patterns of older Americans", Kiplinger "Average Spending by Age").
5. Live on interest only from T-bills/CDs and never touch principal: answered-with-data (Of Dollars and Data "Is $5M in Treasury Bills Enough to Be Set for Life?", with historical income swings).
6. Pay the higher 90%-refundable entrance fee at a continuing-care community or the non-refundable fee: answered-with-data (myLifeSite refund graphs, Mercer Advisors present-value comparison).
7. Contribute a fixed dollar amount or a percent of pay to a 401(k): answered with worked examples (Greenbush Financial, Financial Ducks in a Row); the result matches what viewers already guess.
8. Build a bigger emergency fund at 55+ because job searches run longer: answered-with-data (GAO-12-724T on older unemployed workers, BLS duration figures).
9. Renters relying on Social Security raises to keep up with rent: answered-with-data (articles comparing cost-of-living adjustments with rent growth and buying-power loss).
10. Stuck in an old fixed annuity at a guaranteed 3%: keep it or move to higher current yields: same core as the excluded lock-versus-roll decisions (savings bonds vs T-bills, CD roll vs lock); not pursued.
11. Pension lump sum this year or next as rates move: answered-with-data (annual lump-sum change estimates published each year); also needs mortality tables not available from allowed sources.
12. Open a reverse mortgage line of credit early as a standby: answered-with-data (published standby-line simulations); also overlaps with the parent-gift and home-price candidates.

Merged rather than dropped: buying a retirement home now vs later and selling the family home vs holding it (same home-price-vs-T-bill method as retire-2; not kept to keep viewer groups apart); prepaid tuition for grandchildren (same lock-today's-price structure as retire-1 and belongs with 529 plans in the tax pillar).
