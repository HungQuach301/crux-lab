# retire-3 claim risk: callable 5-year CD at a premium vs a plain 5-year CD

Analyst's guess before running the data: called in about 40% of starts, callable ahead in about 70%. Actual: called in 73.2%, ahead in only 37.9%.

## Phrasings that overstate the claim
- "Banks call 73% of callable CDs": the number comes from a model (Treasury yields as the rate proxy, a 0.25-point call rule, annual call dates), not from records of actual calls.
- "Callable CDs lose money": principal and earned interest are paid back; the shortfall is relative to the plain CD, in points of yield.
- "You'll earn 0.22 points less": that is the historical median of a simplified model; real premiums and call schedules vary.

## The episode must not
- Recommend callable or non-callable CDs, brokers or issuers.
- Forecast whether rates will fall during the term.

## Data limits
- Treasury constant-maturity yields stand in for CD rates; actual CD rates and premiums differ.
- Monthly averages, annual compounding, one-year call protection, reinvestment for the exact remaining term; taxes and fees ignored.
- The 1981-2021 fall in rates weighs heavily; about 14 independent 5-year periods since 1953. History, not a forecast.
