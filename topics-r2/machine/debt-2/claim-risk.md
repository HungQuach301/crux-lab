# debt-2 claim risk

**Phrasings that would overstate the claim**
- "PMI usually comes off in two years." The 23-month median is when the loan reached 80% of a *national index* value. Removal based on current value needs the lender's approval, an appraisal, and often a minimum seasoning and 75% loan-to-value in the early years; at the two-year mark only 15.6% of purchase months were at or below 75%.
- "Your home will appreciate enough." A national index hides local and individual losses; October 2005 buyers took 112 months, and 14.7% of purchase months took over five years.
- "The law removes PMI at 80%." By law the borrower may *request* cancellation when the scheduled balance reaches 80% of original value and it ends automatically at 78% (with conditions such as being current on payments); value-based removal is a lender or investor rule, not the law.

**What the episode must not say**
- No advice ("buy now", "wait for 20%"). Show the schedule, the index range and the failures side by side.
- No forecast of home prices or rates. Say "history, not a forecast."

**Data limits**
- Purchase-only national house price index, not seasonally adjusted, 1991 onward; monthly average of weekly survey rates.
- Insurance premium size, appraisal costs, rent and savings speed are not modeled.
- Sets A and B use only purchase months with enough later data (24 or more than 120 months), so recent buyers are only in set A.
