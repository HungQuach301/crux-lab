# tax-4 claim risk: cash in a Roth IRA vs a savings account

**Phrasings that would overstate the claim**
- "A Roth doesn't help." It does: a median $578 of purchasing power per decade on $7,500 in this model. The claim is only that this is small next to inflation's effect on cash.
- "Savings accounts lose money." They lost purchasing power in about 57% of ten-year windows at T-bill rates, which is not always. The nominal balance never falls.
- "Savings accounts pay the T-bill rate." T-bills are a proxy for the best cash yields. Many bank accounts paid far less, which would make both cases worse.

**What the episode must not say**
- No advice. Do not say "open a Roth", "don't keep cash" or "invest your emergency fund".
- No forecast of rates or inflation.
- Do not imply that Roth earnings can be withdrawn freely. Only contributions can be withdrawn without tax or penalty before the age and five-year conditions are met.

**Data and rule limits**
- TB3MS is the monthly average 3-month bill yield on a discount basis. Compounding it monthly is an approximation.
- A 22% rate is applied to every year since 1934, although actual brackets differed. This is an illustration of today's rate on historical yields.
- The unpublished October 2025 CPI is not used, because windows use January values.
- The rules are quoted from IRS pages: the 2026 IRA limit of $7,500, and that Roth distributions which return regular contributions are not included in income.
