# debt-3 claim risk

**Phrasings that would overstate the claim**
- "Longer car loans don't cost more." They do: about $1,200-1,500 more interest on $35,000. The finding is that the *rate* premium at banks is small. The extra cost comes from the longer term.
- "Your 72-month rate will be lower." The rate was lower in the latest reading and in 14 of 44, but the average was slightly higher. Individual offers depend on credit, lender and dealer.
- "Lenders don't charge more for long loans." Only commercial-bank survey rates were measured. Dealer, captive-finance, credit-union and subprime pricing are not covered.

**What the episode must not say**
- No advice ("take 72 months", "never go past 60"). Show the payment difference against the extra interest.
- No forecast of auto-loan rates or of the premium. Say "history, not a forecast."

**Data limits**
- Quarterly survey of the most common rate at commercial banks, 2015-2026 only (the 72-month series begins in August 2015).
- No used-car rates, no 84-month series, and no fees or add-ons.
- The model does not measure depreciation or negative equity. Longer loans keep the borrower underwater longer, but this data does not show it, so the episode must not claim numbers about it.
