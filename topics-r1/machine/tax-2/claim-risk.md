# tax-2 claim risk: the $500,000 home-sale limit for 2000 buyers

**Phrasings that would overstate the claim**
- "If you bought in Los Angeles in 2000, you'll owe tax." The threshold describes a home that tracked its metro's average index. Individual homes, neighborhoods and remodels differ. Always say "a home that rose like the metro average".
- "Most 2000 buyers now exceed the limit." Unsupported: we do not have the distribution of 2000 purchase prices by metro. Say "the limit is crossed at a 2000 price above about $X".
- "The limit is worth half what it was." Use the measured CPI figure (about $1.05 million in August 2026 prices) and say it is consumer inflation, not house prices.
- Do not convert the threshold into a tax bill for the viewer. Their basis includes improvements, selling costs reduce the gain, and the capital-gains rate depends on their whole income.

**What the episode must not say**
- No advice. Do not say "sell now", "hold until death", "don't downsize" or "use the step-up". The step-up rule (basis = value at death) can be stated as a rule, not recommended.
- No forecast of home prices or of Congress raising or indexing the limit. Pending bills are not law and are not named on the card.
- Do not name the statute or agency on the card. In the episode, cite the sources on screen.

**Data and rule limits**
- FHFA all-transactions indexes (via FRED) are revised every quarter, so values to 2026 Q2 can change slightly at the next release.
- Metro divisions are used where they exist (e.g., Los Angeles-Long Beach-Glendale, not the whole LA MSA).
- The rule set is the joint return with both spouses meeting the use test. Single, surviving-spouse (2-year window) and partial-exclusion cases differ. A single seller's threshold (national index only) is given for reference.
- History, not a forecast.
