# retire: discarded candidates (topics-r1, machine side)

Candidates considered: 19. Kept: 4 (retire-1 annuity 2% raise, retire-2 long-term care 3% vs 5%, retire-3 roll vs lock CDs when 1-year pays more, retire-4 savings bonds that double vs rolling T-bills). Dropped: 15.

1. Delay Social Security 67 to 70 and bridge with savings vs a TIPS ladder: answered-with-data (Advisor Perspectives "Breakeven Real Rates for Delayed Social Security Claiming", Kitces).
2. Retiree cash bucket in T-bills, how often it lost to inflation: answered-with-data (Morningstar "Should You T-Bill and Chill?", rolling 36-month real returns).
3. Turned 62 in 2022, claim now to stop "missing" COLAs?: answered (CNBC "Approaching age 62? What to know about Social Security's 8.7% COLA", Kotlikoff "Ask Larry") with the actual COLA figures; little left for data to add.
4. Claim Social Security at 62 to beat a possible trust-fund cut: the core is a hypothetical scenario with no real data series; forum breakeven calculations already exist (Bogleheads "Social Security reduction and breakeven").
5. Social Security earnings test at 62-66 while still working: answered-with-data (worked examples, e.g. 24/7 Wall St., Vanguard).
6. Taxation of Social Security benefits under fixed thresholds: belongs to the tax pillar and widely answered with inflation-adjusted figures; not searched further.
7. Home equity as the retirement backstop (real home price history): answered-with-data (inflation-adjusted home price histories, e.g. InflationData, Dallas Fed).
8. TIPS vs regular Treasuries for retirees' safe money: answered-with-data (Morningstar "TIPS Look Tempting", Jon Luskin historical tallies).
9. Federal (FERS) workers retiring at the minimum age with no COLA until 62: answered-with-data (FedSmith, FedTools COLA history 1999-2026); also a narrow group.
10. HSA "save receipts and reimburse later" vs medical inflation: not found, but the decision hinges on investment returns that are not available from allowed sources; erosion of the receipt alone does not decide it.
11. HSA planning: do medical prices outrun general inflation? Data showed medical CPI below overall CPI in the last decade, but health-cost trackers already publish this comparison; weak link to a single decision.
12. Rule of 55: keep the 401(k) vs roll to an IRA when leaving work at 55: rule-only, no data series to simulate.
13. RMDs at 73 and whether they drain the IRA: answered with the published tables; weak decision.
14. Roth vs traditional 401(k) based on future tax brackets: no historical bracket series in allowed sources.
15. Starting retirement saving at 25 vs 35 / moving the 401(k) to cash before retiring: needs total-return stock data, which is not available on FRED for long horizons; also heavily answered.

Merged rather than dropped: pension with no cost-of-living adjustment (folded into retire-1); a 5-year lock vs roll check on Treasuries (became retire-3).
