# retire-2 claim risk: 3% vs 5% compound inflation option on long-term care insurance

Analyst's guess before running the data: 3% would fall clearly short (industry claims of 4-5% yearly care cost growth). Actual: 3% compound covered a median 98.1% of the price after 20 years (range 88.9%-104.4%).

## Phrasings that overstate the claim
- "Nursing home costs rise about 3% a year." The measure is the national producer price index for nursing care facilities (what facilities receive, all payers), not the private-pay daily rate a family pays in a given city. Say "a national price index for nursing care facilities".
- "The 5% option is a waste" or "5% over-insures you." The data show 5% compounding outran this index; they do not show that the extra premium is not worth it (local prices, assisted living, home care, premium increases and personal risk are outside the data).
- "3% is enough." It fell short in 55.9% of windows (by up to about 11%).
- Any claim about long-term care costs "doubling" or future prices.

## The episode must not
- Recommend buying long-term care insurance or a specific option or insurer.
- Forecast care prices.
- Treat 136 overlapping windows as independent: they all start between Dec 1994 and Mar 2006.

## Data limits
- Index begins Dec 1994; only about 31 years of history, so roughly one and a half independent 20-year periods.
- Pinned at Mar 2026 (Apr-Aug 2026 values are preliminary and get revised).
- Premiums, premium hikes on in-force policies and benefit triggers are not modeled. History, not a forecast.
