# debt-1 claim risk

**Phrasings that would overstate the claim**
- "T-bills lost to the mortgage" / "prepaying won". Only true at about a 31% federal tax rate or higher. At 22% and 24% the T-bill route was slightly ahead ($285 and $221 on $28,000).
- "It never mattered." The gap depends on the start month. A start in 2023 instead of January 2022 gives a different gap. Say "for this window".
- "Savings accounts" or "high-yield savings". The data is market-average 3-month T-bill yields, not any bank product.
- "Every 3% borrower". The model assumes exactly 3.00%, no itemizing, and $500 at the start of each month.

**What the episode must not say**
- No advice: nothing like "pay down your mortgage" or "buy T-bills". Show the replay and the variables (tax bracket, itemizing, need for cash).
- No forecast of T-bill yields or Fed moves. Do not say "yields will keep falling" (the recent 9-month after-tax dip is history). Say "history, not a forecast."

**Data limits**
- TB3MS is a monthly average on a discount basis. It is converted to a bond-equivalent yield, and actual auction rolls differ slightly.
- Tax is applied monthly at a flat rate. Real tax is paid annually and may cross brackets.
- The model ignores liquidity: cash in T-bills can be used, while extra principal is locked in the house. This matters for the decision but is not measured.
