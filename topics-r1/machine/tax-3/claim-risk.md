# tax-3 claim risk: sell at 11 months or wait for the long-term rate

**Phrasings that would overstate the claim**
- "Waiting loses 18% of the time." Waiting left less after tax in 17.8% of month-long windows of one index over one decade (2016-2026). Always give the window, the index and the period, and say "history, not a forecast".
- "On average you gain $1,654 by waiting." The median gain mostly reflects the market rising during this particular decade, not the tax rule. Say so explicitly. Do not present it as an expected payoff.
- "Your stock" or "your fund": the result is for a position that moves exactly like the S&P 500 price index. A single stock swings much more, so the break-even drop is crossed far more often.
- Use "about" for the break-even drop (2.12% at 24%, 1.65% at 22%). It holds only for a $10,000 gain on a $50,000 position.

**What the episode must not say**
- No advice. Do not say "always wait", "sell now" or "it's worth waiting". Show the trade-off: a fixed tax difference against the observed size and frequency of one-month drops.
- No market forecast ("markets usually rise, so..."). Past windows describe the past only.
- Do not name the index provider on the card. On screen, credit the data source as its licence requires.

**Data and rule limits**
- FRED serves only about ten years of daily S&P 500 closes, and the S&P licence restricts reproduction ("Reproduction of S&P 500 in any form is prohibited except with the prior written permission"). The episode should show derived statistics, not redraw or republish the index series. Confirm the licence position before production. A public-domain alternative may be needed.
- Price index only (no dividends). Windows overlap, so the 2,470 windows are not independent observations.
- Tax setup: single filer inside the 24% (or 22%) bracket, long-term rate 15%, no net investment income tax (MAGI under $200,000), no state tax, losses given no tax value. Other brackets or states change the break-even.
- The holding-period rule ("more than 1 year") is quoted from 26 U.S.C. 1222. The 15% band thresholds quoted are the 2025 figures from Topic 409. For 2026 the band is indexed upward, so the assumption still holds for the 24% bracket.
